Direction 1 — Ray reflects before it reveals

After the last question, before showing any plan, Ray plays back what it actually heard — in natural language. One screen. A few seconds. Then the plan appears.

The moment
"Got it, Y. You want to get stronger — specifically for dance. You've had trouble staying consistent before. Three days a week, working with what you've got. Building your plan now."

This is what a real trainer does after an intake. The word specifically is doing the work — it signals Ray retained the detail, not just the category.

The plan that follows earns more trust because the reflection set it up. Without it, the plan screen is a dashboard. With it, it's a reveal.

Could be text-only or include Ray's blob. Does not need to be long. 3–4 lines. The restraint is part of the confidence.

Direction 3 — Workout preview as a bottom sheet

The plan screen shows the headline and 8-week arc. Then, automatically, a bottom sheet slides up — unavoidable, its own moment. The user doesn't find the workout preview. It finds her.

Why a sheet, not a section
Scrolling to find the preview buries the most compelling proof point. A sheet interrupts — in a good way. It demands the same attention as a notification or a coach tapping you on the shoulder.
The exercise tags
Power · Mobility · Warmup · Core — these aren't just labels. They show the workout has shape and intention, not just a list of things to do. The tags also prime the user for the coaching language they'll hear once the workout starts.

The CTA lives at the bottom of the sheet — "Start your free trial →" — so the decision happens immediately after the most compelling proof point, not after a screen of stats.

The sheet could also be dismissible (swipe down to read more of the plan) — keeping it explorable without burying it.

Direction New — Insight screens between questions

Between certain questions, the onboarding briefly switches to a completely different visual register. Not another question — a full-screen moment where Ray surfaces what it's understood so far.

Different visual language, intentionally
The question screens are structured and functional. The insight screens are atmospheric — a large blob takes over most of the frame, text floats up from the bottom, and tags accumulate to show the profile Ray is building. The shift in register signals: something is happening here beyond form-filling.
The accumulating tags
Each insight screen shows all the tags collected so far, with the newest one highlighted dark. By the third insight, the user can see Ray holding "Dance · Strength · Consistency · Tue/Thu/Sat" — a profile that's visibly theirs, not a template.

This changes what the plan reveal feels like. The user has already watched Ray build her profile in real time. The plan isn't a surprise — it's a confirmation of something she saw forming.

Works best at 2–3 moments in the flow, not after every question. The insight screen earns its place when the answer is genuinely consequential to the plan.

Direction Commitment — woven into the plan

The commitment doesn't get its own screen — it's the closing beat of the plan. After the phases and training approach, Ray makes one direct ask before the button.

Why it works here
The plan has already built the case. By the time the user reaches the bottom, she's seen the phases, the reasoning, the callouts. The commitment lands when belief is highest — not as a separate ask, but as the natural conclusion.
"I'm in →" vs "Start your free trial"
Same action, different emotional register. "Start your free trial" is transactional. "I'm in" is a decision. The date — September 12, calculated from today — makes it specific and real, not a template.

The quote is Ray speaking directly. Short, confident, no hedging. The date pill and session count give it specificity without adding bulk.

The date could be dynamically generated based on the user's schedule — e.g., 30 days from their first available workout day. Makes it feel built for them, not filled in.

Direction Wrapped — The Reveal

After the last question, instead of jumping straight to the plan screen, Ray runs a Wrapped-style sequence — one full-screen card per key insight, each one a beat of recognition before the plan appears.

The arc
"Ray heard it all." → "Dance." → "3 days." → "Consistency." → "Your plan is ready." → Plan screen
Why this works
Spotify Wrapped turns data into a personal mirror. Each card is a moment of recognition — "yes, that's me" — before the reveal. By the time the plan screen appears, it doesn't need to prove Ray understood her. She's already felt it.

The plan screen is the payoff, not the performance. The Wrapped sequence does all the emotional lifting so the plan can be clean and confident without needing to over-explain.

Unlike Spotify Wrapped (annual), this only happens once — at the start of the relationship. It has permission to be a full moment. The cards auto-advance, but could also be tap-to-advance.
