Ray · AI-driven research

A 20-person study that opened Ray’s first B2B pilot

20 → 14recruited, then analyzed
101workouts in four weeks
1B2B pilot, with GoodPath

Opportunity

11% of U.S. adults now take a GLP-1 for weight loss (Gallup, 2026). The drugs bring fatigue, nausea and muscle loss, and providers rarely give patients a plan for staying strong.

The study set out to learn how Ray could better serve GLP-1 users through check-ins, and how to make workouts more accessible on the hard days.

Key findings

Fatigue cost the most workouts, and each user’s week took its shape from their shot day. What users knew about strength training on a GLP-1 varied widely, most walked a lot, and the group chat kept them showing up.

These findings shaped the product direction behind Ray’s first B2B pilot, with GoodPath.

Process

01

A four-week study

20 participants tried Ray for four weeks: two workouts a week, a check-in before each one, a weekly symptom check-in and a group chat. Recruiting ran wide on purpose, and 13 of the 14 analyzed were new to Ray. Seven joined exit interviews.

02

Flag, then a human reaches out

I built an agent in Claude that read every check-in and flagged low readiness, fatigue, dose changes and missed check-ins. It never replied to anyone. I texted each flagged participant myself, and those conversations are where the real findings came from. It also caught two or three participants whose activity didn’t add up.

03

What we learned

Four learnings from the check-ins and conversations, from my readout to the Ray team.

Learning 01

Fatigue costs the most workouts

~30%of workout check-ins mentioned fatigue

65% of weekly check-ins rated workouts “tough but worth it,” and users asked for recovery and nutrition tips.

Learning 02

The shot sets the shape of the week

15%of check-ins involved a dose change

“I take my shot on Mondays, and Tuesday to Thursday I experience a lot of fatigue and muscle aches.” Backloading the week worked for two participants.

Learning 03

Knowledge about GLP-1 varies

2 of 14were told by their doctor to strength train

3 of 14 wanted to know why an exercise was in their plan, and Ray felt less encouraging after week 2.

Learning 04

Users walk a lot

174walks logged by 10 participants

Walks didn’t count toward the plan, and walking replaced workouts that didn’t feel doable that day.

What kept users going

The group was the accountability

Participants loved checking in alongside each other in the group chat. That shared accountability kept them showing up, and it shaped how we improved Ray’s own check-in experience.

“There’s not even a handout or piece of paper I was given when I started.”Study participant, on starting a GLP-1
